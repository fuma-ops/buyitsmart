What matters most for pet hair

01

Brushes that don’t tangle

Classic bristle brushes wrap pet hair and need cutting every week. Rubber or “anti-tangle” rollers keep working on their own.

02

Real obstacle avoidance

Bowls, toys and accidents: only robots with AI or camera-based obstacle avoidance really try to steer around them. Basic models only sense walls and stairs.

03

A self-emptying base

Fur fills a small bin in one run. A base that empties the robot into a bag means you deal with it every few weeks, not every day.

04

Suction where the hair is

Hair hides in rugs. Higher suction with automatic carpet boost helps; on hard floors, mid-range suction is enough.

FAQ

Do robot vacuums get clogged with pet hair?

Bristle brushes often wrap long pet hair. Rubber or anti-tangle brushes reduce this a lot, and a self-emptying base keeps the bin from filling up mid-run.

Can a robot vacuum avoid pet poop?

Only models with AI or camera-based obstacle avoidance try to recognize it, and none is perfect. If you have a puppy or a sick pet, check the floor before a scheduled run.

How often should a robot vacuum run with pets?

Many owners run it daily during shedding season. A schedule plus a self-emptying base keeps fur under control without extra work.
