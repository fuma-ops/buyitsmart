How to choose bed sheets: the 6 things that matter

01

Measure your mattress depth first

The most common sheet problem is a fitted sheet that pops off the corners. Measure your mattress from the bottom seam to the top, including any topper, then choose a pocket depth at least 1 to 2 inches deeper. Standard pockets fit thinner mattresses; “deep pocket” sets fit thicker ones.

💡 Write your mattress depth on your phone: you will need it every time you buy sheets or a mattress protector.
02

Percale or sateen: the weave decides the feel

Percale is a simple one-over, one-under weave: crisp, matte and cool, like a hotel shirt. Sateen floats more threads on the surface: smoother, with a soft sheen and a heavier drape, but it feels warmer and can pill more easily over time.

💡 Hot sleeper? Start with percale or linen. Always cold? Sateen or brushed microfiber will feel cozier.
03

Cotton, linen or microfiber?

Cotton is the all-rounder: breathable, durable and easy to wash, and long-staple cotton feels softer and lasts longer. Linen (made from flax) is very breathable, gets softer with every wash and wrinkles by nature. Microfiber (polyester) is soft, cheap and wrinkle-resistant, but breathes less.

💡 Check the label for “100%” cotton or linen: blends are fine, but they should be priced lower.
04

The thread count myth

Thread count is the number of threads in a square inch of fabric. More is not always better: beyond a few hundred, very high numbers often come from thin multi-ply threads counted several times. Fiber quality and weave matter more than a big number on the box.

💡 For cotton, a mid-range thread count in good long-staple cotton usually beats a very high count in short-staple cotton. Linen is measured differently and rarely shows a thread count.
05

Labels worth trusting

OEKO-TEX Standard 100 means the finished fabric was tested for a list of harmful substances: it is a safety label, not a quality or organic label. GOTS certifies organic fibers and the way they are processed. Check the certificate number on the official website if a brand shows one.

💡 “Organic” on its own is a marketing word: look for a certification and a certificate number.
06

Sleep hot? Wash smart

If you wake up warm, choose breathable fibers (linen, cotton percale) and avoid heavy sateen or microfiber. Wash new sheets before the first night, use warm rather than very hot water, and avoid over-drying: it keeps the fibers soft and limits shrinking.

💡 Two sets per bed is the easy rule: one on the bed, one in the wash, and both last longer.

Bed sheets FAQ

What are the best sheets for hot sleepers?

Breathable fibers and a cool weave: linen or cotton percale. Sateen and microfiber feel smoother or softer but usually sleep warmer.

Percale or sateen: which is better?

Neither is better, they feel different. Percale is crisp, matte and cool; sateen is smooth, slightly shiny and warmer. Choose by how you sleep and the feel you like.

What thread count is best for sheets?

There is no magic number. Fiber quality and weave matter more: good long-staple cotton at a mid-range thread count usually feels and lasts better than a very high count made with thin multi-ply threads.

How do I know if I need deep pocket sheets?

Measure your mattress depth, including any topper. Choose a fitted sheet with a pocket at least 1 to 2 inches deeper than that number.

What does OEKO-TEX certified mean for sheets?

OEKO-TEX Standard 100 means the fabric was tested for a list of harmful substances. It is a product safety label, not a sign of organic fibers or of quality.
